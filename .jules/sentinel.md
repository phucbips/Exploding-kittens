## 2024-05-24 - [Insecure Randomness in Game Mechanics and Identifiers]
**Vulnerability:** The application used `Math.random()` to shuffle cards, deal initial hands, decide where a bomb was inserted into the deck, generate unique identifiers for the cards/game components, and generate IDs for rooms (`generateRoomId`). In addition, `Date.now()` was used for user IDs.
**Learning:** `Math.random()` and `Date.now()` are entirely predictable, which would allow a malicious player to determine the outcome of a shuffle, deduce another player's hand, foresee the location of a bomb, and potentially guess/collide user/room identifiers. Predictability in card games completely compromises fairness and security.
**Prevention:** Always use the Web Crypto API, specifically `crypto.getRandomValues()` and `crypto.randomUUID()` (or their Node.js `globalThis` equivalents), to provide cryptographically secure pseudorandom number generation when making any security-sensitive or fairness-impacting random decisions, like card shuffling or identifier generation.
